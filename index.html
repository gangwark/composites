Which transverse model do you believe?

Reuss assumes fiber and matrix carry the same stress in series, which no real fiber packing does — it is a rigorous lower bound, not a prediction. Halpin–Tsai fits the same two constituents to a semi-empirical form with a geometry factor ξ; ξ→0 recovers Reuss exactly and ξ→∞ recovers Voigt, so it interpolates between the bounds rather than replacing them.

The trap: both formulas want the fiber's transverse modulus. Carbon fiber is strongly anisotropic — roughly 230 GPa along the axis but only ~15 GPa across it. Feeding Ef = 230 GPa into a transverse formula is wrong in both models. Reuss happens to land near the measured value for CFRP because two errors cancel, and that is not a reason to trust it. Glass and aramid are far closer to isotropic, so the two models separate cleanly there — switch the fiber to glass and watch the gap.

Membrane theory spreads the wall stress uniformly through the thickness. The exact elasticity solution does not: under external pressure the hoop stress peaks at the inner surface and the peak exceeds Pr/t by roughly t/2r. At r/t = 10 that is a 5% under-prediction; at r/t = 4 it is over 12%, and it is unconservative in every case. Both reduce to the membrane result as t/r → 0, which the self-test asserts.

How the life integral is evaluated
ΔK = S√(πa) with S = Δσ·Y   (S carries no √π)
N = ∫a₀a_c da / (C·Sm·πm/2·am/2)
m ≠ 2:   N = (acex − a0ex) / (ex·C·Sm·πm/2),   ex = 1 − m/2

Verified against 4-million-step numerical quadrature to 1e−11 relative error for m ∈ {2, 2.5, 3, 4, 5.5}. Folding √π into S and then multiplying by πm/2 again double-counts π and understates life by πm/2 — 5.57× at m = 3.

Equations and their honest limits
σf' ≈ 1.4 σeff  ·  Se ≈ 0.40 σeff   (metals-derived estimates)

Guarded: if σm ≥ σeff the Goodman denominator is non-positive and the component has already failed statically — the tool reports that instead of returning a negative or infinite life.
Stance: a polymer laminate has no true endurance limit and this stress-life model is borrowed from metals. It is here because it is what a first course teaches and because the Fracture panel gives you an independent, mechanism-based second opinion. Where the two disagree, trust the crack.

Hull-tier model — what is real, what is heuristic
Grounded in standard theory:
E₁, E₂, G₁₂, ν₁₂ — Voigt / Reuss micromechanics
E(θ) — classical off-axis compliance transformation; Elam = mean over plies
ηo — Krenchel, mean cos⁴(θ − loading direction)
σ = Pr/t (cylinder hoop) or Pr/2t (sphere), superposed axial & bending
Pcr,sphere = kq·1.21·E(t/R)²  ·  Pcr,cyl = kq·(0.92/(L/R))·E(t/R)2.5
  L/R = 4 is a single named constant shared with the shell-volume calculation, so the buckling model and the mass model can never disagree about how long the cylinder is.
Paris integration — identical kernel to the Bench tier

Declared as heuristic, not textbook:
kq = 0.25 + 0.45·quality — imperfection knockdown tied to void content, misalignment and cure
matrix-cracking and interlaminar-shear margins — ratio estimators, not a Tsai–Wu or Puck solve. They exist to make competing failure modes visible and rank-orderable, and they are honest about being illustrative
cost, CO₂, recyclability, sustainability — index models, useful for tradeoffs, not for procurement

Model Bridge
// interrogate → two models, one kernel · where they must agree, and where they must not

Put both tiers on the same problem

The Bench and Hull tiers are not two versions of the same calculation. They are two modelling stances on one artifact. Copy a geometry across and the shared kernel quantities will agree to the last digit — because they are literally the same function call. Everything that then diverges, diverges for a reason you can name. That list of reasons is the engineering judgement this whole tool exists to teach.

Entrepreneurial mindset — discussion prompts

Curiosity
At what depth does your cylinder's factor of safety drop below 2.0? Drag the depth slider on panel 3 and watch it fall. Now do the same on panel 6 and watch which margin fails first — is it the same one?
Curiosity
Take the flaw size from 1 mm to 5 mm on panel 4. Cycles to fracture do not halve — they collapse. Explain the exponent that does that.
Connections
Panel 9 shows the two tiers disagreeing. Every line of that disagreement is a modelling assumption someone chose. Pick the one you think is most dangerous to get wrong, and say who would have made that call in a real programme.
Connections
Your surrogate's largest feature weight is almost never the variable you would have guessed. Trace it back through the physics until you can explain why.
Creating Value
Design a hull that survives 1000 cycles at 3800 m with FoS ≥ 2.5 and buckling margin ≥ 1.5. Then find a second design that meets the same gates at lower embodied CO₂. What did you trade?
Creating Value
You have budget for exactly one non-destructive inspection method before each dive. What defect size must it resolve? Panel 4 gives you the number — justify it.
Ethics
Stockton Rush said rules are the sign of a poor engineer. You are looking at your own dashboard. Name the single number that most directly contradicts him, and say what it would have cost to fix.
